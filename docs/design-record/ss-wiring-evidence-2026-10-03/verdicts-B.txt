## The three statements (quoted from .gbg/policy/docs-rubric.md)

RULE.DOC.LINKS
  "Cross-referencing is discriminating: a target is linked at most once in one docstring, at its first
  genuine mention."

RULE.DOC.ARGS_FORM
  "Three or more arguments, or any keyword, are listed as bullets under an `# Arguments` heading, where
  several may head one bullet together; fewer are bound in running prose, as in "The arguments are `a`,
  the first, and `b`, the second."; the two are never mixed. A type lists its constructor's arguments so
  too, even where its `# Fields` section repeats them."

RULE.DOC.SENTENCE_OPENER
  "A sentence opens with a capitalised ordinary word, never a lowercase identifier or a formula;
  bullets, comments, headings and fenced code are exempt."

## What the check missed (the FALSE one)

SENTENCE_OPENER, src/ConditionalDensity.jl:245: the check treats the first text after a fenced
```math block as the start of a sentence. Here the display ends in a comma (`R(K) = \int K^2,`) and
the paragraph after it is the apposition that finishes the sentence begun before the fence. A
display-math fence whose last line ends in `,`, `;`, or no terminal punctuation continues the
sentence; only one ending in `.` (or followed by a blank line and a capital) closes it. The check
should carry the sentence state across a math fence instead of resetting it.
